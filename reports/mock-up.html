How Efficiently Do Small & Frontier Models Reason Across Mali’s 13 Languages?

By pairing strict DabaX morphological validation with tokenizer fertility, bits-per-byte compression, and cross-language equity metrics, MALI13 turns Sebeni’s revised baselines into a reproducible comparative yardstick.

Sebeni Evaluation Architecture Preview • Mock-Up Render for benchmark_mali13.md

With clean grammars and 49,957 data-reinforced dictionary entries now active across all thirteen MULTI13 languages, the next milestone is measuring how foundation models—both compact Sebeni-trained checkpoints and frontier commercial APIs—perform on Mali’s linguistic diversity relative to their compute and token budgets.

Beyond Bambara-Centric Averages: A model scoring strongly on Bambara while collapsing on Dogon, Bozo, Hassaniya, or Tamasheq fails Malian digital inclusion. MALI13 pairs the macro headline MALI13-Q with Q_min3 (worst-three-language average) and Q_gap so cross-family disparities are impossible to obscure.

True Cost of Missing African Subwords: Standard byte-level BPE tokenizers frequently shatter Malian extended Latin vowels and Berber clitic chains into single-byte fragments, doubling or tripling sequence length, KV-cache footprint, and API billing on equal semantic content. Section IV isolates this exact Diacritic Penalty.

Unified Local & API Harness: sebeni bench run evaluates local HuggingFace/LoRA adapters (recording exact causal BPB and GPU VRAM) alongside OpenAI-compatible, Gemini, and Anthropic models on identical frozen splits with crash-safe resumable checkpointing and optional K-Veritas sealing.

“Model efficiency in low-resource Africa is two-dimensional: quality per parameter AND tokens wasted per real linguistic morpheme.” — Sebeni MALI13 Design Specification (Mock-Up Preview)
