Lead Dispatch • Multi13 Baseline Overhaul

New Grammars & Guarded Parsing End Phantom Inflation Across 13 Malian Tongues

Ground-up rewrites, 30,936 new dictionary entries, and strict gloss enforcement lift Constitution parse coverage from 15.8%–79.7% to 88.3%–99.6% while exposing legacy engine artifacts.

By the Sebeni Language Engineering Desk • Validated via DabaX & 371 Automated Tests

An exhaustive empirical audit of all thirteen national language baselines in beni/data/baselines/ has fundamentally reshaped Sebeni’s morphological analyzer. Evaluating every language against stratified 300-line samples from beni/data/raw/ alongside parallel texts of the 2023 Constitution of Mali (beni/data/test.json) revealed that earlier raw Φ rewards severely overstated real grammatical understanding.

In extreme cases such as Dogon (dtm), Mamara (myk), and Tamasheq (taq), permissive or reversed stage-0/1 rules split unglossed tokens without matching any dictionary stem—causing daba.newmorph to award stage ≥ 0 and full Φ = 1.0 weight on up to 65.3% phantom parses while genuine dictionary-backed parsing sat between 4.9% and 12.7%.

Meanwhile, four structural flaws broke lexicon indexing silently: missing blank lines concatenated consecutive entries under DictReader; uppercase \ps tags failed to match lowercase grammar rules; village names sat inside \ve; and copy-pasted grammars (such as Fula rules inside Hassaniya Arabic mey) misclassified native words as foreign borrowings (EMPR).

Every non-Bambara grammar has now been rebuilt from raw corpus evidence, topped off by a two-stage Tamasheq multi-clitic and Berber circumfix engine, four-tier data-reinforced dictionaries totaling 49,257 entries, Unicode homoglyph normalization, and DabaX(strict_parse=True) guarding.

“When permissive suffix rules fail dictionary lookup yet return stage 0, raw Φ celebrates noise; strict gloss checking brings true coverage into focus.” — Finding §2.1, Sebeni DabaX Diagnostic Audit

Section II • Comparative Typology Centerpiece

Cross-Linguistic Typology Across Mali’s MULTI13

The thirteen national baselines span three major African language phyla—Niger-Congo (Mande, Senufo, Gur, Atlantic, plus the divergent Dogon family), Songhay (traditionally grouped with Nilo-Saharan), and Afro-Asiatic (Berber and Semitic). Designing a unified parsing strategy required respecting deep typological splits rather than exporting Bambara suffixing assumptions across unrelated families.

The Manding East–West Divide: Corpus evidence established that both kao.txt (Kassonke) and mku.txt (Maninka) predominantly reflect Western Manding morphology (-o/-ŋo definite, -lu/-nu plural, native velar fricative x as in moxo, baxa), whereas test.json["mlq"] blends Eastern and Western traits. Unifying both paradigms eliminated false EMPR flags.

Noun-Class Concord & Consonant Gradation: Senufo (myk, spp), Gur (bmq), and Dogon (dtm) mark animacy and noun-class gender via obligatory nominal suffixes, while Fulfulde (ful) combines 20+ classes and 3-voice TAM with initial consonant mutation (haal/kaal, wiy/mbiy, heɓ/keɓ). Songhay (ses) required housing two coexisting varieties—Koyraboro Senni and Koyra Chiini—inside a single grammar.

Afro-Asiatic Non-Concatenative & Clitic Complexity: Hassaniya Arabic (mey) and Tuareg Tamasheq (taq) invert Niger-Congo suffixing expectations. Both rely heavily on proclitics, broken plurals, and discontinuous consonantal templates; Tamasheq additionally chains up to four clitics (e.g., dăɣ-ăddunya-ta-dăɣ) and Berber circumfixes ({tă||t}, {ti||en}), necessitating two sequential peeling stages in taq/baseline.gram.

Section III • Investigative Engineering Desk

Systemic Architectural & Corpus Diagnostics

§2.1 The daba.newmorph Phantom-Parse Trap

Unguarded lemmatize(word): Initializes stage = -1 and parsedword = [nullgloss(word)]. Whenever a stage 0/1 affix rule splits word into [stem, affix], newparsed != parsedword promotes stage = step even when stem lookup fails (daba.newmorph.parsed(gloss) == False).

Because stage_to_phi assigns full weight 1.0 to every non-EMPR stage ≥ 0, permissive rules—such as Dogon -i/-a/-u, Mamara -a/-i, or reversed Tamasheq prefixes—previously inflated raw Φ to 0.59–0.85 despite genuine parses hovering at 5%–37%.

Global Guard (All 13 Languages) — DabaX(strict_parse=True): Enabled by default with no per-language gate, inspecting every candidate across every language via _is_parsed_gloss(). Any non-EMPR split whose leaf morphemes lack dictionary-backed glosses is demoted to stage = "-1", eliminating phantom parses (0.00%) across all 13 languages.

§2.2 DictReader Failures & Tag Repairs

  • Blank-line record boundaries: daba.formats.DictReader flushes entries only on blank lines (if not line or line.isspace()). Missing blank lines in myk (1,045), snk (961), spp (1,158), and mku caused consecutive entries to glue onto the first headword.
  • PoS-tag case & prose mismatch: spp used uppercase \ps N / V / PRON vs. lowercase :n:; taq used n.m./n.f./adj.; myk used French prose (pron pronom du présentatif).
  • Village leakage in \ve: Because DictReader indexes \ve as headwords, Malian village names in bmq and dialect names in taq polluted the lookup trie until moved to \di.
  • Truncated Dogon lexicon: Active dtm/baseline.dict held only 29 entries while dtm/baseline.dict.txt held 2,252 entries (~150 broken \mm); merging yielded 4,739 clean entries.

Section IV • Implemented Engineering & Lexicon Governance

Safeguards & The Four-Tier Curation Desk

Three Runtime Modules Deployed in PR #1

1. Runtime Homoglyph Normalization & Noise Filtering (beni/utils/normalize.py): normalize_homoglyphs() repairs Greek/Cyrillic encoding drift inside both DabaX.text_processor and SebeniDataLoader (DataConfig.normalize_homoglyphs=True). Opt-in EN/FR commentary filtering (filter_corpus_noise=False default) protects clean training batches without mutating raw files on disk.
2. Global DabaX Phantom Guard & Strict Metric (dabax.py & metrics.py): Applied globally across all languages (strict_parse=True default, no language filter): demotes unglossed non-EMPR splits automatically while MorphologyScorer.strict_parsed_rate() tracks dictionary-backed token coverage alongside Φ.
3. Tier 4 Gloss Curation Utility (beni/utils/curation.py): Mined high-frequency stems carry \ge corpus-stem, \gf radical-corpus, authentic \xv examples, and \rem gloss-pending; freq=N so they remain separate from auto: filtering. curation_summary() and export_gloss_pending() generate frequency-ranked queues for native-speaker linguists.
